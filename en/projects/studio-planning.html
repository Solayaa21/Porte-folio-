Video game localization studio, Paris · 2026

Studio scheduling without double entry

Studio schedules feed a SharePoint database automatically, and a custom Power Apps app lets project managers view, book and export hours, with nothing to re-enter.

The need

Studio bookings lived in Excel Online schedules. To know who was recording what, and how many hours to invoice, everything had to be copied by hand.

What I built

An automatic sync from the schedules to a SharePoint list, a Power Apps app designed around the business, and an export of hours per voice director, sound engineer or project.

What it changes

One single entry, in the tool the teams already know. Hours are ready to invoice, and the same database feeds the production dashboard.

The workflow, step by step
  1. Excel Online schedulesTeams book the studios in their usual schedules. Nothing changes for them.
  2. Power AutomateA sync copies the bookings into a SharePoint list, exactly as they are.
  3. SharePoint listOne database of sessions: studio, project, voice actor, team, times, status.
  4. Power AppsThe project managers' scheduling app: day and week views, session details, manual entries.
  5. Hours exportAn Excel workbook ready to invoice, generated in one click by Power Automate and an Office Script.
The app
  • Schedule of the five studios by day and by week, colored by project or by status.
  • Four indicators for the period shown: booked hours, projects, voice directors and sound engineers involved.
  • Session details: project, studio, voice actor, characters, team, times and notes.
  • Manual entry of a session outside the schedules, with a conflict warning, plus team absences.
  • Direct access to the production dashboard from the app.
  • Hours export: current month, previous month or custom dates, filtered by project or studio. Only confirmed bookings count.
  • Two versions of the app: one for project managers, one for voice directors.
What makes it reliable
  • The list stays an exact mirror of the schedules: a deleted booking also disappears from the database. Manual entries and public holidays are handled separately.
  • Daily backup of the lists, kept in SharePoint.
  • SharePoint rather than a heavier database, as long as volumes allow it: fewer licenses and less maintenance.
  • Internal owners for every app and every flow, so everything keeps running without me.
  • Tests on the export logic (statuses, dates, overlaps, multiple roles) before going live.
